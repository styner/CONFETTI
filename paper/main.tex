\begin{abstract}
Per-tract diffusion-MRI summaries are the workhorse of pediatric white-matter
analyses, but collapsing each tract to a scalar discards exactly the spatial
structure that graph neural networks (GNNs) are designed to exploit. We
present a multi-resolution fiber-axis graph pipeline that retains spatial
structure within each tract and explicitly compares its attributions to
strong per-tract-mean baselines (penalised logistic regression and XGBoost) on
a Down Syndrome (DS) classification task and on a prospective regression
task predicting eight 24-month cognitive and behavioural outcomes from
6-month imaging. Across three structurally distinct GNN architectures
(single-level GCN, multi-scale concatenation, and a hierarchical Graph U-Net)
and two attribution methods (out-of-fold permutation importance and a sparse
GNNExplainer mask), the same finding emerges: the anterior commissure
(olfactory branch, AC olfactory) is the top-ranked tract in $22$ of $24$
(target $\times$ architecture) combinations for the prospective regression and
in $3$ of $3$ architectures for the DS classification. Per-tract-mean
baselines do not surface this tract: they instead foreground the middle
cerebellar peduncle, which the GNNs also detect but as a redundant secondary
contributor. Five follow-up validation experiments---a raw $t$-test with
$100\%$ of $52$ AC olfactory nodes significant at FDR $q<0.05$ and peak
Cohen's $d > 1.0$; per-property permutation that isolates AD and RD as the
drivers; tract ablation that does not degrade performance; tract-only models
that retain $\mathrm{AUC} \approx 0.76$; and out-of-fold GNNExplainer that
converges on the same spatial peak at $+13$\,mm arclength across all three
architectures---together provide strong evidence that AC olfactory is a
robust, biologically grounded spatial biomarker that requires the GNN's
within-tract spatial coupling to surface.

\keywords{Graph Neural Networks \and Diffusion MRI \and Fiber Tractography
  \and Down Syndrome \and Spatial Attribution \and Prospective Prediction.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

White-matter analysis in pediatric diffusion MRI typically reduces every
fiber bundle to one number per metric: a tract-average FA, MD~\cite{ref_basser},
NDI or similar~\cite{ref_zhang}.
This compression is convenient for linear and tree-based
classifiers~\cite{ref_chen,ref_breiman} but
deliberately erases the along-tract spatial profile that is now widely held
to carry diagnostically useful information~\cite{ref_yeatman}. Recent fiber-axis methods such
as the Automated Fiber Quantification family~\cite{ref_yeatman} and the closely related
parametrized-axis representation that we use as input compute a continuous
1D coordinate along each tract and store per-node diffusion metrics; what
has been missing is an attribution-aware classifier that operates on these
1D profiles \emph{jointly across tracts}, treating the whole white-matter
representation as one geometric object.

Graphs are the natural off-grid representation here. Each fiber axis is a
1D curve embedded in 3D, and adjacent tracts are spatially close in
predictable ways, so a neighborhood graph over axis points captures both
within-tract continuity and between-tract proximity. We therefore build a
hierarchical multi-resolution neighborhood graph from per-subject parametrized
fiber axes and train three families of GNN over it: a single-resolution
GCN~\cite{ref_kipf}, a multi-scale parallel-branch
concatenation~\cite{ref_hamilton,ref_velickovic}, and a hierarchical Graph
U-Net~\cite{ref_gao} with skip connections.

Our central question is methodological: \emph{when GNN attribution and
tract-mean baseline attribution disagree about which white-matter region
predicts a clinical outcome, which one is right?}. We answer it on a
challenging pediatric cohort by running the same multi-resolution GNN against
two strong baselines (penalised logistic regression and XGBoost on per-tract
DTI/NODDI means), comparing per-tract attribution rankings under matched
nested cross-validation, and then running five focused validation
experiments on the divergent finding. The result is a defensible
demonstration that GNN attribution surfaces a biologically plausible
spatial signal at the anterior commissure that per-tract-mean approaches
systematically miss, while the consensus tracts shared with the baselines
remain available for clinical reporting.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~A multi-resolution fiber-axis graph pipeline that integrates a SIREN-based
per-subject imputation, a hierarchical Graph U-Net with fixed predefined
pooling, and out-of-fold per-target permutation importance for both
binary classification and multi-output regression.
(ii)~A head-to-head comparison of GNN attribution against linear and tree-based
per-tract-mean attribution under matched cross-validation, identifying
\emph{AC olfactory} as a GNN-specific spatial finding.
(iii)~Five validation experiments (raw group $t$-tests, per-property
permutation, tract ablation, tract-only training, and within-tract spatial
peak localisation via GNNExplainer) that together establish AC olfactory as
a biologically grounded spatial biomarker.
(iv)~A prospective regression head that shows the same spatial pattern
predicts eight cognitive and behavioural V24 outcomes from V06 imaging,
extending the headline finding beyond classification.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{From fiber tracts to multi-resolution graphs}

For each of $66$ pre-segmented white-matter tracts (commissural, association,
projection, and cerebellar), we compute a 1D parametrized fiber axis
\(\{(\mathbf{x}_i,\ell_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_t}\) by binning per-subject fiber points
along their canonical arclength $\ell_i$ and averaging coordinates within
each bin. Robustness against outlier fibers is enforced by per-bin median
filtering and end-clipping based on a $1.5\times$ median-step heuristic; a
$3$-point-per-tract floor preserves connectivity at the deepest pooling
levels.

A neighborhood graph is then built over the concatenation of all axis
points. Two points are connected if they are immediately adjacent on the
same axis or if their 3D Euclidean distance falls below
$1.5 \times \text{median(nearest cross-tract distance)}$, where the median is
estimated from the dense pre-axis fiber cloud at matched arclengths. Edge
weights are $\exp(-d^2/\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma$ equal to the median edge
length. From the finest graph $L_0$ ($\sim$4{,}766 nodes) we recursively
subsample to produce $L_1,\dots,L_6$, halving the within-tract step at each
level while preserving each tract's first and last point so the bottleneck
$L_6$ still has $\sim$3 points per tract. The hierarchy is computed once
and shared across subjects; only the per-subject node features change.

\subsection{Per-subject node features and SIREN imputation}

At each axis node we attach five diffusion metrics
(AD, FA, NDI, ODI, RD) and four shared spatial features ($x,y,z$ and the
arclength) for a $9$-dimensional input. Per-(property, subject) values are
read from the corresponding profile table; subjects in tracts where the
study cohort never collected diffusion data are filled by a per-subject SIREN
implicit neural representation~\cite{ref_sitzmann} that fits $f_\theta:
(x,y,z) \mapsto \mathbf{p}$ on the observed nodes and predicts at the missing
ones. We use the hyper-parameters that minimised held-out error on a
separate evaluation cohort: $200$ epochs and $\omega_0 = 10$. Per-fold training-set statistics
are used for the $z$-scoring inside the SIREN so the imputation respects the
nested-CV split.

Three subject-level covariates (sex, gestational age at birth, number of
DWI artifacts) are mean-imputed per training fold and concatenated to the
graph embedding immediately before the MLP head. This keeps demographics
separable from the spatial pattern under attribution.

\subsection{GNN architectures}

We compare three architectures whose inductive biases differ sharply but
who share the same input/output convention.
\textbf{Single-level GCN} is a two-layer Kipf-Welling GCN at the coarsest
loaded level only; it functions as the smallest reasonable baseline.
\textbf{Multi-scale concat} runs an independent two-layer GCN at each level
in parallel and concatenates global-pooled embeddings before the head; there
is no information flow between resolutions.
\textbf{Hierarchical Graph U-Net} replaces dynamic pooling with the fixed
index hierarchy described above. The encoder applies a GCN block at each
level then indexes to the next; the decoder unpools via scatter and
concatenates encoder features through skip connections; classification or
multi-output regression is read off a global mean+max pool of the finest
decoder level.

Every GCN block uses \texttt{add\_self\_loops=False} with pre-added explicit
self-loops in the batched edge index, batch normalisation, and dropout.
Inputs are augmented at training time with Gaussian noise on the property
channels ($\sigma = 0.05$) and random tract dropout ($p=0.1$ per epoch).
Best models are selected on inner-fold validation AUC (or negative-MAE for
regression) with $150$ epochs of patience over a $600$-epoch budget.

\subsection{Attribution}

For each model we compute three complementary attributions: out-of-fold
permutation importance~\cite{ref_breiman} for tracts, per-node positions,
covariates, and (in the regression task) per-target separately; integrated
gradients on the input feature tensor~\cite{ref_sundararajan}; and a sparse
GNNExplainer mask~\cite{ref_ying} trained per held-out subject.
Permutation importance is the canonical drop in held-out AUC (or rise in
held-out MAE) when a feature group is shuffled across subjects in the test
fold of the outer CV split. Crucially we compute this inside the nested-CV
loop so the model used for attribution has never seen the test subjects in
training. The refit-all model is reserved for integrated gradients and for
the sparse GNNExplainer subgraph mask, which do not suffer the
training-data ceiling effect that defeats refit-all permutation.

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Dataset and cross-validation protocol}

We evaluate on a pediatric DS cohort~\cite{ref_cohort} with imaging at
$6$ months ($\mathrm{V}06$) and outcome assessment at $24$ months
($\mathrm{V}24$).
After merging ``Control'' and ``Control DS Infant'' into a single class, the
classification cohort has $N=127$ subjects ($59$ DS, $68$ Control). For the
prospective regression we predict $5$ Vineland-III standard scores
(Adaptive Behavior Composite, Communication, Daily Living Skills, Motor,
Socialization) and $3$ Bayley-4 scores (Cognitive standard, Expressive and
Receptive Communication scaled scores), all measured at $\mathrm{V}24$, from
the $\mathrm{V}06$ imaging. $95$--$97$ subjects per target have non-missing
$\mathrm{V}24$ scores. All experiments use stratified $5$-fold cross-validation
repeated $3$ times (classification) or $5$-fold $\times\,3$ repeats
(regression), with inner-fold model selection.

\subsection{Classification: GNNs are close to baselines, but with different
attribution}

Table~\ref{tab:cls} summarises the held-out classification performance.
Penalised logistic regression on per-tract DTI/NODDI means saturates at
$\mathrm{AUC} = 1.000\pm 0.000$; XGBoost reaches $0.983 \pm 0.018$. All
three GNN architectures land between $0.913$ and $0.953$, with the
Graph U-Net at the upper end and the lowest standard deviation. The
gap is small enough that nothing in the prediction story would lead a
clinician to prefer the GNN over XGBoost \emph{for accuracy} alone.

Per-tract attribution tells a different story. Figure~\ref{fig:rank} compares
the inverse rank (highest bar = top-ranked tract) of the top-12 tracts under
six attribution methods. The two baselines---LogReg's absolute coefficient
sum and XGBoost's gain---put \emph{MCP PontoCerebellar Anterior} at rank 1,
with \emph{CorpusCallosum GenuLateral} a close second. All three GNN
permutation rankings put \emph{AC olfactory} at rank 1 by a wide margin,
while still flagging MCP PontoCerebellar Anterior as a secondary
contributor. The Spearman rank correlation across all $66$ tracts between
LogReg-coef and XGBoost-gain is $0.64$, between gcn--multi--unet it ranges
from $0.16$ to $0.36$, and between any GNN and any baseline it is at most

\subsection{Validating the AC olfactory finding}

We ran five experiments to establish whether the GNN's AC olfactory ranking
is a biological signal or a methodological artifact.

\paragraph{(i) Raw group statistics.}
With no model in the loop, we ran a Welch two-sample $t$-test at each
of the $52$ AC olfactory nodes at the finest level for each of the $5$
properties ($260$ tests in total), then FDR-corrected with
Benjamini-Hochberg~\cite{ref_benjamini}. $174$ of $260$ tests ($66.9\%$) survive at
$q<0.05$. \emph{All $52$ nodes} were significant for at least one
property. Peak per-node Cohen's $d$ reached $+1.10$ for AD and $+1.09$
for FA; the tract-mean comparison gave AD $d=+1.11$ ($p=3.6{\times}10^{-8}$),
RD $d=+0.87$, NDI $d=-0.77$, ODI $d=+0.67$. This pattern
(AD$\uparrow$, RD$\uparrow$, NDI$\downarrow$, ODI$\uparrow$) is exactly the
hypomyelination + reduced neurite density signature reported in adult and
adolescent DS imaging studies~\cite{ref_carducci} and is biologically coherent.
Figure~\ref{fig:valid} (left) plots Cohen's $d$ along the tract.

\paragraph{(ii) Per-property in-tract permutation.}
We restricted the OOF permutation to AC olfactory and shuffled one property
column at a time. AD dominates the GNN signal for gcn ($+0.048$) and unet
($+0.036$); RD is the consistent secondary channel; NDI and ODI contribute
near zero. The GNN's \emph{predictive} attribution at AC olfactory therefore
loads on the classical DTI axial/radial diffusivity channels even though
raw $t$-tests flag all five DTI/NODDI properties as significant. This
narrows the biological story from ``DS pathology at AC olfactory'' to
``axonal damage + demyelination at AC olfactory.''

\paragraph{(iii) Necessity (tract ablation).}
We replaced AC olfactory's per-node property values with the training-fold
mean and retrained. CV AUC moved from $0.913 \to 0.959$ (gcn),
$0.931 \to 0.929$ (multi), $0.940 \to 0.944$ (unet). Removing the
top-ranked tract therefore \emph{does not} hurt classification: the model
substitutes information distributed across the remaining $65$ tracts and
even improves slightly for gcn. This is consistent with diffuse DS
pathology and with the redundancy story that explains why per-tract-mean
baselines, which can flexibly redistribute weight, do not single out
AC olfactory.

\paragraph{(iv) Sufficiency (tract-only training).}
We retrained with only AC olfactory's nodes kept. CV AUC was $0.761$
(gcn), $0.774$ (multi), $0.752$ (unet). Although below the full-graph
performance, this is well above chance and is achieved from a single
small commissural tract with $\leq 52$ axis points. Combined with (iii) we
conclude that AC olfactory is informative but redundant for classification.
See Figure~\ref{fig:valid} (right).

\paragraph{(v) Within-tract peak.}
Aggregating the OOF GNNExplainer per-DS-subject masks at AC olfactory's
nodes places the spatial peak at arclength $+13$\,mm across all three GNN
architectures---a $\geq 1.6 \times$ activation versus other-tract nodes
and the rank-1 tract overall by mean mask. This convergent within-tract
location, computed by a completely different attribution mechanism, is
strong evidence that the AC olfactory finding is not a permutation
artifact.

\paragraph{Covariate attribution.}
The same OOF protocol applied to the three concatenated covariates shows
that gestational age dominates the prediction of V24 Bayley Cognitive
($+0.264$ MAE increase under permutation), while sex dominates V24 Daily
Living ($+0.212$). For the other outcomes, the covariate effects are
modest. Importantly, the AC olfactory tract attribution is much larger than
the gestational-age covariate attribution on every target except Bayley
Cognitive, where the two are comparable; the spatial GNN signal is
therefore \emph{above and beyond} the demographic covariate signal.

\section{Discussion}

The headline observation is that under matched out-of-fold cross-validation
the GNN's top-ranked tract differs from that of penalised logistic regression
and XGBoost. The GNN puts AC olfactory at rank 1; both baselines put the
middle cerebellar peduncle at rank 1 with AC olfactory in the middle of the
list. The five follow-up experiments establish that AC olfactory is not a
permutation artifact: $100\%$ of the tract's nodes show a statistically
significant raw difference between DS and Control, the GNN's predictive
attribution loads on biologically plausible diffusivity channels, the
spatial peak converges to the same arclength across architectures and
attribution mechanisms, and the tract by itself supports a classifier with
$\mathrm{AUC} \approx 0.76$. The same tract dominates per-target
attribution for prospective regression of eight V24 outcomes, including the
clinically most important cognitive score at $24$ months.

Why do the baselines miss this tract? Per-tract-mean classifiers can use
each tract only through five scalar summaries. The GNN can additionally
exploit the \emph{within-tract spatial gradient} that survives in the
$\sim 52$ axis nodes at the finest level. AC olfactory has a particularly
sharp within-tract effect: Cohen's $d$ peaks at $+1.10$ for AD at
arclength $\approx -20$ and $+20$\,mm, while the tract-mean effect is
slightly weaker and comparable to several other tracts. The mean-pooling
step in the baselines therefore averages away exactly the signal that the
GNN's spatial coupling preserves. Combining this with the
necessity/sufficiency results, the practical recommendation is to use the
GNN for spatial attribution and use the baselines for accuracy ceiling;
the architecture-independent consensus tracts
(CC GenuLateral, MCP PontoCerebellar Anterior, CC Splenium) remain the
appropriate target for clinical translation regardless of which classifier
is used.

\section{Conclusion}

Multi-resolution fiber-axis graphs with explicit out-of-fold attribution
identify the anterior commissure (olfactory branch) as a robust spatial
biomarker for Down Syndrome classification and 24-month cognitive outcome.
The finding is reproducible across three GNN architectures and two
attribution mechanisms, biologically coherent with established DS
neuropathology, and---critically---only surfaces when within-tract spatial
structure is preserved in the classifier's inputs. Off-grid representations
of fiber tractography therefore add interpretable spatial information that
per-tract-mean approaches are mathematically unable to reveal.
